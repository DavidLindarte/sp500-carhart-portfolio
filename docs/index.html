S&P 500 factor portfolio: screening, Carhart and quadratic optimisation

A long-only US$10M portfolio of S&P 500 stocks. Each quarter the pipeline screens every constituent for a significant Sharpe ratio and a stable beta, estimates expected returns with the Carhart four-factor model, builds a covariance matrix, and solves a quadratic programme with sector caps. It is then held to the next rebalance and judged out of sample against SPY.

Pipeline

  1. Universe: current S&P 500 constituents with GICS sectors. Daily adjusted prices (Yahoo Finance) as log returns. Carhart factors from the Ken French data library.
  2. Screening: Lo (2002)-adjusted Sharpe-ratio significance, positive beta, and beta stability (Chow / Quandt–Andrews break tests).
  3. Expected returns: a Carhart four-factor regression per stock, with VIF checks and a ridge fallback. Compared against CAPM and Fama–French 3 using out-of-sample RMSE and Diebold–Mariano tests.
  4. Risk: the sample covariance when the estimation window has enough observations, otherwise a factor-model covariance Ω = BΣFB′ + D.
  5. Construction: QP with cvxpy: long-only, 25% sector cap, L1 penalty λ chosen by sensitivity analysis.
  6. Policy: estimation window and rebalance frequency chosen on a separate 2-year comparison period (adopted: 3-year window, 63-day rebalancing). The walk-forward never touches that period.
